# Debt

## Description

**Debt** is borrowing value from the future to use in the present — with the obligation to repay, typically with interest. Financial debt is the most familiar form, but the mental model extends to technical debt (deferred code quality), organizational debt (deferred cultural or process problems), social debt (favors owed), and health debt (deferred physical maintenance). All forms share the same structure: present consumption at the cost of future obligation, often with compounding costs.

- **Origins:** Financial debt is ancient — Mesopotamian clay tablets record loans from 3000 BCE. As a mental model extending beyond finance, it was popularized by software engineers who coined "technical debt" (Ward Cunningham, 1992) and by thinkers like Ray Dalio, whose *Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises* (2018) provides the most comprehensive framework for understanding debt cycles.

- **Key Principles:**
    - **Leverage amplifies both gains and losses:** Debt multiplies the return on equity when things go well — and multiplies losses when they go badly. The same leverage that enables spectacular wealth creates spectacular bankruptcy.
    - **Compounding works against you:** Interest compounds over time. Small debts ignored become large obligations — in finance, relationships, and code.
    - **Debt brings forward consumption:** All debt does is pull future purchasing power into the present. There is no free consumption — someone always pays, either through future repayment or default.
    - **Technical debt:** Shortcuts taken in software development create accumulated complexity that slows future development. Like financial debt, it compounds — small shortcuts create maintenance burdens that grow exponentially.
    - **Eating your seed corn:** A metaphor for debt's danger: consuming next year's planting stock for immediate hunger guarantees worse hunger next year.
    - **Debt service as fixed cost:** Once incurred, debt creates mandatory future outflows that reduce flexibility and can become existential during downturns.

- **Real-World Examples:** A startup using venture debt to extend runway — productive if growth materializes, catastrophic if it doesn't. A codebase with years of technical debt — each new feature takes 10x longer because of accumulated complexity. A country running persistent fiscal deficits that eventually trigger a debt crisis. A person deferring difficult conversations until the relationship debt becomes irrecoverable.

---

## When to Use

- **Evaluating leverage decisions:** Before taking on any form of debt, explicitly model the downside scenario — can you service the debt if things go worse than expected?
- **Technical debt triage:** Periodically audit accumulated technical, organizational, and relational debt. Name it, quantify it, and decide consciously whether to address or continue accumulating.
- **Identifying hidden obligations:** Look for implicit debt — promises made, standards deferred, relationships neglected — that are accumulating without being tracked.
- **Long-term resource planning:** Ensure that present consumption levels don't systematically consume future capacity.

---

## When to Avoid

- **Refusing all debt categorically:** Productive debt — borrowing to invest in assets that generate returns exceeding the interest rate — creates value. The danger is unproductive debt, not debt per se.
- **Over-applying the metaphor:** Not all deferred action is "debt." Some decisions are better made later when more information is available — that is optionality, not debt.

---

## Thinking Steps

1. **Identify the debt:** What is the present obligation? What is the future repayment structure?
2. **Calculate the true cost:** Interest, fees, opportunity cost of collateral, flexibility lost — what is the all-in cost of this debt?
3. **Model the downside:** If conditions worsen, can you service this debt? What is the failure mode?
4. **Evaluate the return:** What does borrowing this resource enable? Does the expected return exceed the cost of debt?
5. **Check for hidden debt:** Are you accumulating technical, organizational, social, or health debt alongside financial debt? Inventory it.
6. **Establish a repayment strategy:** For productive debt, confirm you have a credible repayment plan that doesn't rely on optimistic outcomes.
7. **Audit periodically:** Review all forms of accumulated debt and make conscious decisions about addressing it before it compounds further.
