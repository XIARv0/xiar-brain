# Multiply by Zero

## Description

**Multiply by Zero** is a mental model derived from the mathematical identity: any number multiplied by zero equals zero, regardless of how large the other numbers are. Applied cognitively, it describes how a single critical failure — a fatal flaw, a broken dependency, a zero-probability threshold — can nullify all other strengths, effort, and investment. It is the model of single points of failure.

- **Origins:** The mathematical principle is elementary, but its application as a decision-making framework was popularized by Shane Parrish and Charlie Munger, who both emphasize identifying existential risks and fatal flaws before investing effort. The concept overlaps with engineering reliability theory (single points of failure), security thinking (a chain is as strong as its weakest link), and Warren Buffett's insistence on avoiding catastrophic losses over maximizing gains.

- **Key Principles:**
    - **One zero destroys everything:** A business with stellar product, team, and market fit fails if it runs out of cash. A relationship with love, trust, and shared values collapses if one party is dishonest. A strategy with excellent execution fails if the premise is wrong.
    - **Asymmetry of negatives:** Unlike other weaknesses, zero-producing failures are not compensated by strengths elsewhere. A 10 × 10 × 10 × 0 = 0, always.
    - **The fatal flaw test:** Before investing in optimization, ask: is there anything about this situation that, if present, would make everything else irrelevant?
    - **Pre-mortem as zero detection:** Imagining a future failure and asking "what single thing went to zero?" is a structured way to find zero-producing risks.
    - **Checklist for zeros:** In complex projects, explicitly list the non-negotiables — the items that if absent or failed would guarantee failure. Address these first.

- **Real-World Examples:** A startup with everything right except legal compliance — one lawsuit zeros the company. A bridge designed beautifully except for a corroded load-bearing bolt. A medical treatment that works for 99% of cases but kills 1% — the expected value calculation must account for the irreversibility of the 1%. An investment thesis correct on all dimensions except the market timing — zero.

---

## When to Use

- **Evaluating any multi-factor plan or investment:** Before assessing strengths, identify whether any single factor could produce zero.
- **Pre-mortem analysis:** Ask "what single thing, if it went wrong, would destroy this entirely?"
- **Risk triage:** Prioritize eliminating zero-producing risks over optimizing non-critical factors.
- **Due diligence:** In any major decision, explicitly search for the fatal flaw that makes all other analysis irrelevant.
- **Personal decisions:** Applying to jobs, entering relationships, or choosing partners — identify non-negotiables that, if violated, no other positive factor compensates.

---

## When to Avoid

- **When no true zero exists:** Not all weaknesses are fatal. Use this model only for genuinely existential risks, not ordinary challenges.
- **When it paralyzes action:** Searching for zeros too aggressively can produce paralysis. Identify and address the plausible zeros, then proceed despite residual uncertainty.

---

## Thinking Steps

1. **List all the key factors in your plan, investment, or decision.**
2. **For each factor, ask: "If this fails completely, can the other factors compensate?"**
3. **Identify the zeros:** Which factors, if they go to zero, destroy the entire outcome?
4. **Rank by probability × irreversibility:** A high-probability zero is urgent. An irreversible zero is existential.
5. **Address zeros before optimizing:** Don't spend time polishing 10s if there's an unresolved 0 on the list.
6. **Run the pre-mortem:** Imagine the project failed disastrously. What single thing went to zero?
7. **Create explicit safeguards:** For each zero, define the mitigation — redundancy, insurance, contractual protection, or go/no-go criteria.
8. **Accept residual risk:** Once identified zeros are addressed, proceed — total zero elimination is impossible.
