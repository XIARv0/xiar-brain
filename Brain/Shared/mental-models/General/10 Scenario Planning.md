# Scenario Planning

**Scenario Planning** is a strategic foresight method used to make decisions in environments characterized by high uncertainty and complexity. Instead of attempting to predict a single, definitive future, scenario planning develops a set of plausible, alternative futures (scenarios) to help organizations stress-test their strategies and identify robust paths forward.

- **Origins:** The model was pioneered during the Cold War by **Herman Kahn** at the RAND Corporation to model nuclear outcomes. It gained significant corporate prestige in the 1970s via **Pierre Wack** at **Shell Oil**, who famously used scenario planning to help the company navigate the volatility of the 1973 oil crisis while competitors remained caught off guard.
    
- **Key Principles:**
    
    - **Plurality:** We move from "The Future" (singular) to "Futures" (plural).
        
    - **Uncertainty vs. Predictability:** Distinguishing between "predetermined elements" (trends we can reasonably forecast) and "critical uncertainties" (variables that could swing wildly).
        
    - **Narrative Logic:** Scenarios are not just data; they are stories that illustrate the "how" and "why" of different outcomes, making them intuitive for human decision-makers.
        
- **Real-World Example:** Shell's team created multiple scenarios regarding global energy supply and demand decades before the oil shocks of the 70s. When prices spiked, they weren't shocked; they had a "manual" ready for that specific reality.
    

---

## When to Use

- **Strategic Pivots:** When an organization is entering a new market or industry where historical data provides little predictive value.
    
- **Capital Investment:** For high-stakes, long-term decisions (e.g., building infrastructure) that cannot be easily reversed if initial assumptions fail.
    
- **High Volatility:** When external forces—such as geopolitical shifts, technological disruption, or regulatory changes—create a "fog of war" that makes single-point forecasting dangerous.
    
- **Organizational Alignment:** When different stakeholders have fundamentally different views on how the future will evolve; scenario planning forces a constructive debate on those diverging worldviews.
    

---

## When to Avoid

- **Stable/Routine Environments:** If you are operating in a highly regulated, predictable environment, the effort of scenario planning is unnecessary and adds expensive complexity.
    
- **Short-term Tactical Planning:** Do not use this for next-week operations. It is a long-horizon tool meant to shape vision, not immediate task lists.
    
- **The "Prediction" Trap:** Avoid the pitfall of thinking any one scenario is the "correct" one. The goal is preparation, not accurate fortune-telling.
    
- **Lack of Executive Buy-in:** If leadership is wedded to a single dogma, scenario planning will be treated as an academic exercise rather than a decision-making tool.
    

---

## Thinking Steps

1. **Define the Focal Issue:** Select a specific decision or time horizon (e.g., "The future of our supply chain over the next 10 years").
    
2. **Identify Drivers of Change:** List all factors that could influence the outcome (e.g., technology, policy, consumer behavior, climate).
    
3. **Rank by Uncertainty and Impact:** Filter these drivers. Keep those that have a high impact on your focal issue but are highly uncertain.
    
4. **Develop the Matrix:** Choose the two most critical and independent uncertainties to serve as axes of a 2x2 matrix, creating four distinct "quadrants" or scenarios.
    
5. **Flesh Out the Narratives:** Write a descriptive story for each quadrant. What does the world look like? What are the key characteristics of this future?
    
6. **Stress-Test Strategies:** Take your current strategy and apply it to each scenario. Ask: "Would we survive or thrive here?"
    
7. **Identify "No-Regret" Moves:** Isolate actions that are beneficial across _all_ scenarios. These become your immediate strategic priorities.
    
8. **Monitor Indicators:** Define "signposts"—key data points that will alert you to which scenario is currently unfolding in the real world.
